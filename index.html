Methodology & Operational Framework

1. Objective and Horizon

This tracker estimates the probability that a major technology equity or major stock index falls 50% or more from its price today over a rolling, constant 2-year forward window (T = 2.0 years). Rather than tracking a fixed expiration date that decays toward zero, the evaluation window rolls forward continuously so that daily tail-risk readings remain directly comparable over time.

2. Tracked Assets and Benchmark Index ETFs

The dashboard tracks leading enterprise and consumer tech equities (NVDA, GOOG, MSFT, AAPL, AMZN, META, TSLA) and special purpose vehicles (SPCX), alongside the three major US equity market benchmarks via their primary exchange-traded funds (ETFs):

  • SPY: SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (tracks the S&P 500 Index).
  • QQQ: Invesco QQQ Trust (tracks the Nasdaq-100 Index).
  • DIA: SPDR Dow Jones Industrial Average ETF Trust (tracks the 30-stock Dow Jones Industrial Average).

Because these funds track the underlying indices on a continuous 1:1 basis, evaluating option-implied tail risks on the ETFs directly reflects the market-implied probability of a systemic 50% market drawdown.

3. The Dynamic Risk-Free Rate and Forward Price

The risk-free interest rate (r) is dynamically mapped to the current market yield of the 2-Year US Treasury note (Yahoo ticker: 2YY=F). Aligning the discount rate with the exact 2-year duration automatically reflects macroeconomic shifts, from the 0.2% rate environment of 2021 to the higher yield regime of 2023–2026. Forward prices incorporate this rate along with a baseline dividend yield approximation (q = 0.5%).

4. Volatility Estimation: Historical Monthly Proxy vs. Live LEAPS Regime

Because historical options chain quotes are not stored in free public APIs, the dataset is divided into two distinct operating regimes:

  • Historical Monthly Series (January 2021 – September 2025): Evaluated on the 15th of each month. Historical realized volatility is computed over a rolling 63-trading-day window (~3 months). Because option markets persistently trade at a premium to realized volatility, the volatility proxy is scaled by a Volatility Risk Premium (VRP = 1.12).
  • Live Daily Tracking (Starting October 2025 Onward): Every weekday after market close, the automated engine scrapes live LEAPS options chains from Yahoo Finance, extracting at-the-money (ATM) implied volatility directly from active calls and puts.

5. Downside Skew Modeling

Deep out-of-the-money puts trade at an implied volatility premium over ATM options (the volatility smile/skew). Rather than fitting sparse or illiquid deep-OTM put contracts, the model scales the liquid ATM baseline by an empirical downside skew factor of 1.18 to determine the wing volatility for the 50% drawdown strike.

6. Risk-Neutral Tail Probability Q(S ≤ K)

The risk-neutral probability Q reflects the market price of downside catastrophe insurance embedded in option contracts. Evaluated via the Black-Scholes lognormal CDF with a Breeden-Litzenberger skew slope factor (0.85), Q reflects investors' risk aversion and appetite for portfolio insurance without assuming expected positive stock price growth.

7. Physical Tail Probability P(S ≤ K) with CAPM Drift

The physical probability P represents the statistical real-world probability of a 50% drawdown. Under the physical measure, equities have positive expected drift (μ) estimated via the Capital Asset Pricing Model (μ = r + β × ERP, with an Equity Risk Premium of 4.5%). Because equities are expected to appreciate over a two-year holding period, the statistical physical probability is significantly lower than the risk-neutral hedging probability.
