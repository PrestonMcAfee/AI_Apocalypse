Methodology & Theoretical Framework

Objective & Horizon: This dashboard estimates the probability that a constituent equity suffers a 50% drawdown ($S_T \le 0.5 S_0$) over a rolling, constant 2-year forward horizon ($T = 2.0\text{ years}$). Evaluating on a fixed-duration rolling window removes seasonal calendar expiration decay and maintains uniform comparability across tickers.

Risk-Neutral Probability $\mathbb{Q}(S_T \le K)$: Extracted from forward option pricing under the risk-neutral pricing measure. Rather than relying on illiquid, sparse deep-out-of-the-money put quotes, the model anchors to high-liquidity at-the-money (ATM) LEAPS contracts and applies an empirical equity volatility skew ($\sigma_{\text{wing}} \approx 1.18 \times \sigma_{\text{ATM}}$) to price the 50% strike. Tail probability is evaluated via the closed-form Black-Scholes lognormal CDF $d_2 = (\ln(F/K) - \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\text{wing}}^2 T)/(\sigma_{\text{wing}}\sqrt{T})$ with a Breeden–Litzenberger (1978) skew-adjustment factor. $\mathbb{Q}$ reflects the market price of downside insurance and embeds investors' risk aversion; it does not assume expected positive equity returns.

Physical (Real-World) Probability $\mathbb{P}(S_T \le K)$: Estimates the objective statistical probability by augmenting the distribution with a positive drift rate estimated via the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM): $\mu = r + \beta \times \text{ERP}$, where $r \approx 3.9\%$ (2-year Treasury yield) and $\text{ERP} = 4.5\%$ (equity risk premium). Because equities have an expected positive drift over a 2-year horizon, $\mathbb{P}$ is substantially lower than the risk-neutral probability $\mathbb{Q}$.
